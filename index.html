Read this first: source, HFT extensions & correctness

Based on Data Structures in Practice by Danny Jiang, licensed CC BY 4.0. These are adapted summaries, not the complete book. The HFT examples, questions, drills, and engineering caveats are new study material; they are not claims or endorsements by the author.

The source focuses on systems, firmware, and embedded software, not exclusively trading. Its benchmark numbers and hardware rules are not universal. This guide deliberately strengthens several points: C++ inter-thread synchronization needs atomics, reservation is not publication, lock-free is not wait-free, and a Bloom positive is never proof of membership. No strategy, speed, or hard-real-time guarantee is implied.

Suggested route: 1-4 for measurement and layout; 6-8 and 13 for queues, lookup, and ownership; 5 and 9-12 for order-book structures; 14 and 18-20 for production engineering. Use each drill to test an assumption on your own target hardware.
